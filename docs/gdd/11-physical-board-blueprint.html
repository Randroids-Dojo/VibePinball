11 Physical Board Blueprint

Status: partial

Spec

This file translates the supplied real pinball reference photo into a physically plausible Silverball Social board architecture. It copies the mechanical density, shot staging, device layering, and readable physical composition of a real machine. It must not copy licensed character art, names, decals, or exact theme assets from the reference photo.

Physical scale

The canonical playfield uses a real-machine scale before camera framing. One game unit represents 2.5 inches. The playable area is 8.1 units wide by 16.8 units long, matching an approximately 20.25 inch by 42 inch modern playfield. The current x axis remains left to right. The current z axis remains top negative and apron positive. The playfield surface is y 0. The ball radius is 0.2125 units, which maps to a 1.0625 inch pinball.

Part Physical size Game units Implementation note
Ball diameter 1.0625 in 0.425 Use a Rapier sphere radius of 0.2125 to 0.24 units, keeping the current radius acceptable.
Flipper bat 3.0 in 1.2 Use tapered capsule or compound colliders, not a rectangular box.
Lane clearance 1.35 to 1.75 ball widths 0.58 to 0.74 Inlanes, outlanes, and return lanes must be narrow enough to read as real gates.
Orbit clearance 1.6 to 2.0 ball widths 0.68 to 0.85 Side orbit guides need two curved wall rails plus rollover sensors where appropriate.
Ramp lane width 2.0 to 2.5 ball widths 0.85 to 1.06 Ramp floors should have side walls, entrance lips, and explicit exit handoffs.

Board zones

The board should be built as five physical zones so future slices can replace sparse procedural geometry with stable authored devices.

Zone Bounds Purpose Reference cue
Apron and drain z 6.4 to 8.4 Apron, trough entry, center drain, ball save messaging, shooter feed. The lower printed apron and central drain below the flippers.
Lower playfield z 3.1 to 6.4 Flippers, slingshots, outlanes, inlanes, rubber posts, lane rollover switches. Dense lower guide plastics and two main flippers.
Mid playfield z -1.8 to 3.1 Target bank, lock entry, ramp mouths, return feeds, central posts. Central standup bank and raised red bumper caps above it.
Upper playfield z -6.2 to -1.8 Pop bumper nest, top lanes, skill shot, upper orbit handoffs. Triangular pop bumper nest and top rollover region.
Shooter lane x 3.35 to 4.05 Plunger launch, skill-shot tuning, one-way entry to upper lanes. Right-side launch lane wrapping to the top arch.

Component decomposition

The table must be authored from named device records that can produce render meshes, Rapier colliders, sensors, lamp state, and rule events from one source of truth.

ID group Physical components Collider contract Sensor and event contract Render contract
boundary.* Side walls, top arch, shooter divider, apron rails, rebound rubbers. Fixed wall segments. Curves are approximated by short tangent cuboids or convex hulls. No scoring events. Drain and trough sensors are separate. Metal side rails, black inner walls, visible glass rim.
lane.lower.* Left and right outlanes, left and right inlanes, rollover wire switches, divider posts. Two wall rails per lane, post colliders at each gate, rubber rings on exposed posts. lane events for inlane, outlane, and return rollover IDs. Lit arrow inserts, rollover wires, plastic guides above lane mouths.
flipper.* Two main bats, pivot posts, end rubber, return stops. Joint-driven kinematic bat or motorized rigid body with capsule-like collision. No direct score event. Flipper state is input state and physics state. White or cream bats with colored rubber and metal pivot caps.
sling.* Left and right triangular slingshot rubbers. Angled rubber wall with active impulse normal away from the sling face. sling events with side ID and cooldown. Triangular plastic, two posts, pulsing lamp under plastic.
pop.* Three pop bumpers in a triangular nest. Circular skirt sensor plus fixed round cap collision. Impulse radiates from center. bumper events for pop-a, pop-b, and pop-c. Red bumper caps, chrome rings, lamp pulse on hit.
target.bank.* Five central standup or drop targets, not just three spaced boxes. Thin vertical face collider with small rear stop and hit deflection. target events with hit, lit, and completed states. Individual decals, lamp inserts below each target, completion flash.
lock.saucer Central saucer or scoop behind the target bank. Bowl entry walls, capture sensor, hold point, eject vector. lockEnter, lockHeld, and lockEject events once rules support them. Visible saucer cup, lit "lock" insert, ball visibly held or represented.
ramp.left Left ramp entrance, raised ramp floor, yellow-style wireform return to left inlane. Inclined floor collider, side rail colliders, entrance lip, elevated return rail. rampMade at entrance and exit, with combo eligibility from exit. Transparent ramp at the mouth, bright wire return above the playfield.
orbit.right Right orbit lane wrapping around upper playfield into a green-style wireform return. Curved side-wall chain, upper gate, return rail, exit guide to right inlane. orbitMade and top-lane rollovers where the ball crosses the arch. Raised wire guide that visually traces the ball path.
shooter.* Shooter lane, plunger rod, top one-way gate, skill-shot rollover lanes. Narrow fixed walls, plunger impulse, one-way gate represented as angled guide geometry. skillShot when the charged launch lands in the lit top lane. Plunger housing, lane groove, lit top lane target.

Shot map

The table must give each flipper multiple readable shots. These are physical ball paths, not only scoring concepts.

Shot Primary flipper Path Outcome
Left orbit Right flipper Right flipper to left side lane, around top arch, across the lit top-left rollover arrow, through bumper entry or full orbit. Feeds upper bumpers, awards orbit when completed, can chain to right return.
Left ramp Right flipper Right flipper through left ramp mouth, up raised lane, onto left wireform return and lit left inlane arrow. Awards ramp, returns safely to left inlane, starts combo timer.
Center target bank Either flipper Direct shot into five standups below the bumper nest. Advances lock qualification and rebounds dangerously toward lower playfield.

Right orbit Left flipper Left flipper to right side orbit, around upper right guide, into return wireform and lit right inlane arrow. Awards orbit or lights jackpot during multiball.

Right outlane drain None Lower right outlane rollover and lit outlane arrow through the right apron guide into the center drain mouth. Records the side-drain hardware path and feeds drain handling.

Layering and height

A real board is not flat art with pegs on top. The implementation must separate physical layers so the camera sees depth and the ball can travel under or over devices where appropriate.

Layer Y range Contents Collision behavior
Playfield deck 0.00 to 0.08 Painted wood surface, inserts, lane art, decals. Static floor with low friction. Inserts are visual or sensor overlays.
Ball center 0.2125 to 0.24 Ball rigid bodies. Dynamic spheres with capped speed and tuned restitution.
Low devices 0.15 to 0.55 Flippers, rubbers, targets, posts, slings, bumpers. Primary gameplay collision layer.
Plastic covers 0.45 to 0.75 Decorative plastics and lamp covers. Mostly visual. Add collision only where a plastic physically contains the ball path.
Ramps 0.18 to 0.95 Ramp floors, entrance flaps, rail sides. Elevated floor and rail collision. Transition points must be explicit.
Wireforms 0.85 to 1.45 Raised metal return rails and habitrail paths. Constrained path collision with clear entrance and exit handoffs.

Data architecture

The next implementation slice should introduce a declarative board definition instead of scattering coordinates through render and physics modules.

TableBlueprint
  id: "silverball-social-v1"
  scale:
    inchesPerUnit: 2.5
    playfieldWidth: 8.1
    playfieldLength: 16.8
    ballRadius: 0.2125
  devices:
    boundaries: BoundarySegment[]
    posts: Post[]
    rubbers: RubberSegment[]
    lanes: Lane[]
    rollovers: RolloverSensor[]
    flippers: FlipperDevice[]
    slings: SlingDevice[]
    bumpers: PopBumper[]
    targets: TargetBank[]
    saucers: Saucer[]
    ramps: RampPath[]
    wireforms: WireformPath[]
    inserts: LampInsert[]
  shots:
    ShotPath[]

Suggested module ownership:

  • src/game/tableBlueprint.ts: declarative component records, IDs, dimensions, and shot paths.
  • src/game/physicsTable.ts: converts blueprint records into Rapier bodies, colliders, sensors, and device impulses.
  • src/game/renderTable.ts: converts the same records into Three.js meshes, lamps, plastics, rails, ramps, and debug overlays.
  • src/game/rules.ts: consumes normalized device events, never raw collider coordinates.
  • tests/tableBlueprint.test.ts: validates IDs, clearances, lane widths, shot endpoints, and sensor coverage.

Acceptance criteria

  • The board contains at least two full lower inlane and outlane pairs, two slings, two main flippers, a central drain, and shooter lane.
  • The upper playfield contains three pop bumpers, three top rollover lanes, and at least one orbit path that can feed the bumpers.
  • The mid playfield contains a five-target bank and a readable lock saucer or scoop.
  • The left ramp and right orbit each have elevated return geometry, visible rails, entrance sensors, exit sensors, and safe inlane feeds.
  • Every physical scoring device has a stable ID shared by render, physics, rules, lamps, and tests.
  • Every lane has two wall boundaries, a rollover or entry sensor where relevant, and a measured clearance compatible with the ball radius.
  • No major shot is represented only by flat art. Shots must have colliders, sensors, and visible physical guides.
  • The reference photo inspires mechanical layout density only. Final art, characters, names, and decorative IP are original Silverball Social assets.
